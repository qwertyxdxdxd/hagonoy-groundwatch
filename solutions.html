Mitigation & Solutions

We can't undo subsidence that has already happened, but we can slow it down and adapt. Here's how.

The key idea: Use less groundwater

Experience from cities around the world, such as Tokyo, Shanghai, and Bangkok, shows that when groundwater pumping is strictly reduced and replaced with surface water, subsidence slows dramatically. The most important solution for Hagonoy is the same: pump less from the ground and recharge more.

Community actions

Save water at home

Fix leaking faucets and pipes, turn off taps while brushing, reuse rinse water for plants and cleaning, and avoid running pumps longer than needed.

Harvest rainwater

Collect rainwater from roofs in drums or tanks to use for washing, flushing, gardening, and cleaning, which reduces pumping from wells.

Don't dig new deep wells

Connect to piped water from the local water district where available, and register existing wells.

Keep waterways clean

Garbage clogs canals and esteros. Join barangay clean-up drives so floodwater can drain faster.

Plant and protect mangroves

Mangroves reduce wave energy, trap sediment, and protect coastal barangays from storm surge.

Observe, report, share

Report sinking structures, new cracks, and worsening floods. Talk to family and neighbors about subsidence.

Sustainable practices

Mitigation (slowing subsidence)

  • Shift from groundwater to surface water (rivers and dams) for public supply
  • Regulate and monitor wells, and close illegal or unnecessary ones
  • Recharge aquifers through ponds, permeable surfaces, and protected green areas
  • Monitor subsidence with satellite radar (InSAR), GPS, and benchmarks
  • Reduce water losses in distribution pipes

Adaptation (living with it)

  • Elevated, flood-resilient houses and schools
  • Dikes, floodgates, and pumping stations
  • Mangrove belts and nature-based coastal defenses
  • Early warning systems for high tides and storm surge
  • Land-use planning that avoids building in the most exposed areas

Be ready: What to do during floods

While we work to slow subsidence, floods will keep coming. Knowing what to do before, during, and after a flood keeps families safe.

  • Monitor advisories. Follow PAGASA weather bulletins and high-tide schedules, and the Hagonoy LGU's official announcements.
  • Prepare a go-bag: drinking water, ready-to-eat food for 3 days, flashlight, radio, extra batteries, power bank, first-aid kit, medicines, and important documents in a waterproof bag.
  • Raise valuables. Move appliances, documents, and electronics to higher shelves or the second floor.
  • Know where to go. Find your barangay's evacuation center and the safest route to reach it.
  • Clear drainage around your home so water can flow away.
  • Plan with your family. Agree on a meeting place and who checks on elderly neighbors and persons with disabilities.
  • Evacuate early when authorities advise. Don't wait for water to rise.
  • Switch off the main power before water reaches outlets, but only if you are standing somewhere dry.
  • Avoid walking or driving through floodwater. Fast-moving water just ankle to knee deep can knock people off their feet, and it can hide open manholes and debris.
  • Stay away from electric wires and posts.
  • Keep children out of floodwater. Don't let them swim or play in it.
  • Stay informed through a battery-powered radio or your phone, and keep your phone charged.
  • Return home only when authorities say it is safe.
  • Have an electrician check your wiring before turning the power back on.
  • Boil drinking water or use treated water until your supply is confirmed safe.
  • See a doctor if you waded through floodwater, especially with wounds. Leptospirosis can be prevented with early treatment.
  • Clean and disinfect floors, walls, and items that touched floodwater, and wear boots and gloves.
  • Remove standing water in containers, tires, and gutters to stop mosquitoes that spread dengue.

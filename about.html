About Land Subsidence

What it is, how it happens, and why Hagonoy is especially vulnerable.

What is land subsidence?

Land subsidence is the gradual settling or sudden sinking of the Earth's surface caused by the movement of materials underground. It can happen naturally, but in many coastal and delta cities around the world it is driven mainly by human activity, especially the over-pumping of groundwater (Herrera-García et al., 2021).

Unlike an earthquake, subsidence is usually slow and silent. A few centimeters a year is hard to notice day to day, but over 10 to 20 years it can lower the ground by a meter or more. In a low-lying coastal town, that is the difference between dry streets and permanent tidal flooding.

How does it happen?

  1. Groundwater is pumped from sand and gravel layers (aquifers) through deep wells.
  2. Water pressure drops. Underground water helps hold up the weight of the soil above it. When it is removed, that support is lost.
  3. Clay layers squeeze. Water slowly drains out of the clay between aquifers, and the clay particles pack together more tightly.
  4. The surface sinks. The compaction adds up across all the layers and shows up as a lower ground surface.
  5. It is mostly permanent. Once clay has compacted, it does not bounce back even if the water returns. Stopping over-pumping can slow or stop further sinking, but lost elevation is rarely recovered (Galloway et al., 1999).

Natural vs. human-induced subsidence

Natural causes

  • Natural settling and compaction of young river and delta sediments
  • Tectonic movement and earthquakes
  • Dissolving of underground rock (sinkholes), which is rare in Hagonoy

Natural compaction in deltas is usually slow, on the order of millimeters per year.

Human-induced causes

  • Excessive groundwater extraction, the dominant cause around northern Manila Bay (Rodolfo & Siringan, 2006)
  • Heavy loads from buildings, landfill, and road raising on soft soil
  • Draining wetlands and converting land use

Groundwater pumping is the leading cause of subsidence in many parts of the world and can lower the ground far faster than natural compaction (Galloway et al., 1999).

Why is Hagonoy especially vulnerable?

Low elevation

Much of the town lies only about a meter or two above mean sea level, so even small amounts of sinking matter.

Soft delta soils

Hagonoy sits on the Pampanga River delta. Its thick, young clay deposits compress easily when groundwater is removed.

Heavy groundwater use

Households, businesses, and aquaculture in the region have long depended on deep wells for their water supply.

Facing Manila Bay

Coastal barangays are exposed to high tides, storm surges, and sea-level rise.

Many waterways

Rivers and creeks carry upstream floodwater from the Pampanga River basin through town on its way to the bay.

Frequent typhoons

Monsoon rains and typhoons combine with high tides to cause severe and long-lasting floods.

Overview: Land subsidence in Hagonoy

Hagonoy is a low-lying coastal municipality in western Bulacan, on the northern shore of Manila Bay. Much of the town sits on soft river and delta sediments, just above sea level, and is crossed by rivers, creeks, and fishponds.

For decades, communities, businesses, and fishponds around northern Manila Bay have relied heavily on groundwater pumped from deep wells. When more water is pumped out than nature can replace, the clay layers underground compress and the land surface sinks. Studies of the area have reported sinking of several centimeters per year in some coastal places, much faster than the global rise in sea level (Rodolfo & Siringan, 2006; IPCC, 2021).

The result is something many residents already feel: high tides now reach streets and homes, floodwater takes longer to drain, and families must keep raising their floors and roads.
